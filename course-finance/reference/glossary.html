Money Glossary

Every word you need to know about money — in simple language!

This glossary is your cheat sheet! Every time you hear a money word and forget what it means, come here to look it up. 🔍

💰 Money
Something everyone agrees to use to swap for things they need or want. It comes as coins (metal) or bills (paper).
Example: "I used money to buy an apple at the shop."
→ Lesson 1: What is Money?
🔄 Trade
To swap something you have for something someone else has. Long ago, people traded instead of using money.
Example: "I'll trade you my apple for your sandwich!"
→ Lesson 1: What is Money?
🟤 Penny
The smallest coin. Worth 1 cent (¢1). It is brown/copper in colour.
Example: "A bubblegum costs 5 pennies."
→ Lesson 2: Coins and Bills
⬜ Nickel
A silver coin worth 5 cents (¢5). Equal to 5 pennies.
Example: "I have 1 nickel — that's the same as 5 pennies!"
→ Lesson 2: Coins and Bills
⬜ Dime
The smallest looking coin but worth 10 cents (¢10). Equal to 10 pennies or 2 nickels.
Example: "A dime is tiny but worth 10 cents!"
→ Lesson 2: Coins and Bills
⬜ Quarter
A silver coin worth 25 cents (¢25). Equal to 25 pennies.
Example: "I need 4 quarters to make one dollar."
→ Lesson 2: Coins and Bills
💵 Bill (Note)
Paper money. Bills come in $1, $5, $10, $20 and more. Worth more than coins.
Example: "A $5 bill is worth five dollars."
→ Lesson 2: Coins and Bills
💪 Earn
To get money by doing work or helping others. You work → you get paid.
Example: "I earned 3 coins by sweeping the floor."
→ Lesson 3: Earning Money
🧹 Chore
A helpful job you do at home — like making your bed, sweeping, or tidying toys. You can earn money for doing chores.
Example: "My chore is feeding the cat every morning."
→ Lesson 3: Earning Money
✅ Need
Something you MUST have to stay healthy and safe — like food, water, clothes, and shelter.
Example: "Food is a need — without it, I'd get very sick."
→ Lesson 4: Needs vs Wants
🌟 Want
Something nice to have but you can live healthily without it. Wants make life fun but aren't necessary for safety.
Example: "A toy dinosaur is a want — fun but not needed!"
→ Lesson 4: Needs vs Wants
🐷 Saving
Keeping money instead of spending it, so you have more later. The money grows the longer you save it.
Example: "I put 5 coins in my piggy bank to save them."
→ Lesson 5: Saving vs Spending
🛍️ Spending
Using money to buy something right now. Once you spend it, it's gone.
Example: "I spent 2 coins on an apple juice."
→ Lesson 5: Saving vs Spending
🐷 Piggy Bank
A safe, special container (often shaped like a pig!) where you keep your saved coins.
Example: "I drop my coins in my piggy bank each week."
→ Lesson 5: Saving vs Spending
🎯 Savings Goal
Something specific you decide to save up to buy. You work out the price and save a little each week until you have enough.
Example: "My savings goal is a telescope that costs 20 coins."
→ Lesson 6: Saving for a Goal
⏳ Delayed Gratification
Waiting for something better later instead of getting something smaller right now. A very powerful skill!
Example: "Zara waited 5 weeks and got the telescope she really wanted."
→ Lesson 6: Saving for a Goal
❤️ Give
To share some of your money with people or animals who need help. Part of the three-jar system.
Example: "I put coins in my Give jar to donate to the animal shelter."
→ Lesson 7: Spend · Save · Give
🏦 Three-Jar System
A simple way to manage money using three containers: SPEND (for now), SAVE (for later), GIVE (for others).
Example: "When I got 9 coins, I put 3 in each jar."
→ Lesson 7: Spend · Save · Give
